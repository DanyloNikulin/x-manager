# Format: single post

A post is the account's position on one thing, argued in the account's register. It is not an annotation of the source, and it is not the source's figures with a punchline. `tweets` stays empty.

## The test

Delete every number and every name from the draft. What remains must still be a position someone could disagree with. If what remains is a quip, the draft was a summary with a punchline. Rewrite it.

## Structure

1. The claim: what the account thinks is going on. A statement about how things work, not a report that something happened.
2. The reasoning: the mechanism, or the contradiction, that makes the claim true. This is most of the post.
3. The evidence: at most one figure, with its unit and what it is a share of, and its source URL when the brief requires sourced numbers. The figure supports the claim; it is not the claim.
4. The consequence: what follows, what the coverage misses, who it lands on. The twist is the last sentence. Do not explain it.

At least two thirds of the sentences are the account's own reasoning. At most one third carries facts from the source.

## Where the opinion comes from

Open the source before writing: with the web fetch tool when one is available, otherwise from `source_notes`. Read it the way the account would. What does the piece take for granted? What does it leave out? Who is not in the story? Which number does the work, and which one is decoration? The post argues with the piece, or past it. It never retells it.

## Shape, shown (voice comes from the brief, not from here)

Annotation, wrong: "The city bought 400 electric buses. 62% arrived late. 18% failed inspection. Somebody signed for them anyway. example.org/buses"

Argument, right: "A late bus is a procurement problem, not a battery problem. The city bought its fleet on a delivery date nobody priced, so the penalty clause was the cheapest thing to waive, and 62% of the buses turned up late for free. The contract was the vehicle. example.org/buses"

## Budget

- Hard limit: 280 weighted characters. A URL counts as 23.
- Working band: 200 to 280. The worker measures every draft; under 200 comes back once for a rewrite with the number. The band is for the argument. Reaching it with a second and a third figure is the wrong way to reach it.
- Short sentences are the voice. Short posts are not.

## Do not

- Stack figures. "X sessions. Y% of Z. W% of those." is a table, not a post.
- Retell the article and add a one-liner that could sit under any news.
- Open with the source's numbers. Open with the claim.
- Hedge, moralise, ask the reader a question, explain the joke, use hashtags or emoji.

## The brief's samples

Sample posts in the brief show temperature and diction, not length and not depth. Match the temperature. The band wins on length, the brief wins on voice.
